## Content
14. Objection I Cant Afford This.mp4 I can't afford this. Cool, what makes it unaffordable? Let's try to get that data out of the person. I can't afford this. Great, does that just mean that you don't have the cash or the credit lines available? Because we can get you approved for some funding offers that we have. Are you open to getting this financed in the instance that we can get you approved? And then start going through the funding options with them. Use tools like coachfinancing. com, finfi. com, F-I-N-F-I. com. You could potentially have other funding partners. To be clear, if you're a salesperson working with one of our clients, or if you're a student or a client watching this, we have a referral partner. His name is Giovanni. He does an incredible job at getting people approved for funding for 0% interest credit cards. He gets them approved for about $150,000 on average. And it's 0% interest credit cards where the person doesn't have to make a single interest payment for 12 months. They can just pay down the principal and they have an entire year to do so. There's all kinds of solutions out there. Talk with the people about that. Make sure you have those options available in your business in order to be able to close the most deals. Because if somebody says, 'I can't afford this,' it's either, once again, they don't have the capital in their account currently to be able to pay for it. Or they might have enough cash, but they can't justify making the lump sum payment to you and then have enough left over to do everything else they want to do. Or they just didn't know you have funding options. I would encourage going through the funding options. If they still say, 'No, I'm not really interested in doing the funding options,' you know, what else do you think I could do? Maybe you have some internal payment plans that you could go over with the person. So in my business, as an example, for our information product education company, we have a 5K product. We give the most incentive to people who pay it in full in cash. The people who provide funding options and get approved for it, we'll give them the same incentives for the people that get approved. For the 5K, because we still get paid 5K if they go through the funding option, they're going to pay a little more in interest, but they're going to have a much smaller payment for a longer period of time, and they're once again making those payments to the funding company, not to us. And then, if the person does not get approved for funding, and if once again, they don't have $5,000 in cash, then we have our own internal payment plans, like a $2,600 split pay, where they'll pay it over two months instead, with $2,600 a month. At one point, we even had a quad pay. To be fair, we no longer offer it, but it was four payments at $1,325. So there's always plenty of solutions that you can offer to people. Just make sure you actually have those options available. That way, when somebody says, 'Hey, I can't afford this,' you have a long list of all the options that you can walk them through to make sure they can easily afford it instead.
