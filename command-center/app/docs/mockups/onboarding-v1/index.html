The five originals, for reference

1

Mission Control

One screen. No tabs, no client picker. Every client is a row showing progress and what is blocking them; the row opens in place with all four sections side by side. Forms that came in are rows too, just recessed until you act on them.

Kills the pickerKills the tabsKeyboard driven → 2

The Board

The eight real pipeline phases as columns. A client physically sits in the phase they are stuck in, so nothing has to be read to know who is where. Column headers carry the SOP's own timings, and a card that has sat too long turns red. Clicking one opens its checklist beside the board.

Who is stuck whereDrag to advanceAging built in → 3

The Queue

Stops being about clients and becomes about work. One list of the next actions across every client, ordered by what is holding a launch up. You never pick a client, you work down the list and it empties. Waiting-on-them and the self-ticking checks are kept separate so they cannot be mistaken for your job.

Zero navigationRanked by blockersNudge buttons → 4

The Cockpit

Three permanent columns. Clients in a rail on the left with progress rings, the checklist in the middle, and everything that decides Go live pinned on the right where you can watch it while you tick. Switching client is one click and never leaves the page. The QA nests under its own step.

Nothing hiddenLive checks always visibleClosest to today → 5

The Runway

Onboarding is a seven-day pipeline with timings written into the SOP, so it is drawn as a schedule. Each client runs left to right from the day they signed; the solid bar is where they are, the hollow marker is where they should be. Underneath, the exact step that stopped the clock and how long it has been stopped.
