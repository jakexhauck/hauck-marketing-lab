How online ads actually work

Stripped to its bones, every ad campaign is the same six-step machine. We'll use a pizza shop as the example, but the same machine runs for a window cleaner, a plumber, or any other local business.

SECTION 01

The six pieces of any ad campaign

WHY IT MATTERS

Every campaign — whether you spend $10/day or $10,000/day — is built from the same six pieces. Memorise them once and you'll never feel lost on a strategy call again.

THE BUDGET

You pay Meta — Facebook and Instagram's parent company — to show your ads. Think of it as paying someone to put flyers on doors, except the flyer also chases the person around the internet for a few days.

TARGETING

You pick who sees the ad — location, age, interests. Meta does most of the heavy lifting from there. Narrow targeting feels safer but usually costs more; broader audiences let the algorithm find buyers you'd never have picked yourself.

THE CREATIVE

The ad itself — image, video, headline, body text, call-to-action. This is the flyer. Nine times out of ten, when an account is broken, the creative is what's broken.

THE CLICK

When someone taps the ad. Most people won't. That's normal — a 1–3% click-through rate is healthy on a cold audience.

CONVERSION

When someone does the thing you actually want — calls, books, or fills out a form. The ad's job ends at the click; the landing page and offer have to finish the sale.

ROAS

Return on ad spend. What you got back vs. what you spent. The whole point of running ads. Every other number is a clue; ROAS is the verdict.

SECTION 02

What the funnel looks like on $10/day

THE SHAPE

Most people who see the ad won't click. Most who click won't convert. That's the shape of every funnel, not a problem to fix. Your job is to keep the top wide and the bottom honest.

THE NUMBERS
THE SPEND $10 spent on the day
THE REACH 500–1,000 people see the ad impressions
THE CLICKS 15–25 tap the ad clicks
THE LEADS 3–5 turn into leads
CUSTOMERS 1–2 become paying customers
NORMAL RANGE

A 1–2% conversion rate at the bottom of this funnel is healthy. Below 0.5% and something upstream — usually the offer or the landing page — is leaking.

THE TAKEAWAY

If each customer is worth $40+, that's $80+ back on $10 spent. An 8× return — and that's before factoring in repeat purchases or referrals.

SECTION 03

30 days of real numbers

THE ACCOUNT

Here's what a healthy account looks like at the one-month mark — pulled straight from a window-cleaning client running at $10/day. Nothing cherry-picked; this is a typical month two.

THE PROOF
$312spent
$4,200revenue back
13.5×ROAS
31,400people reached
847clicks
47leads
COMMON MISTAKE

Looking at week one and panicking. The first 7–10 days are the algorithm's learning phase — costs are inflated, conversions are sparse. Judge an account at day 30, not day 3.

KEY POINT

A 13.5× ROAS isn't magic — it's a small budget, a clear offer, and one creative that resonates. Most local businesses fail at the offer, not the ads.
