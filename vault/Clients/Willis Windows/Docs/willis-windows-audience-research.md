Sir. Audience deep-dive for Willis Windows, weighted against the three competitor briefs.

## 1. Top 10 emotional reasons they buy

1. **Hosting panic.** Graduation party, baby shower, Mother's Day brunch, the in-laws driving in from Ohio. The windows are the last thing on the list and they need it done THIS week.
2. **The light hit different.** First sunny morning after a Michigan winter, sun comes through the kitchen slider, and they finally SEE the film, dog nose smears, pollen crust. It's been there for months. Now they can't unsee it.
3. **Pride of the block.** New neighbors moved in with a spotless house. Now theirs looks like the dumpy one. Suburban status anxiety in Birmingham, Rochester Hills, Northville.
4. **The view tax.** They paid for the lake view, the golf course view, the wooded backyard. Can't enjoy what they paid for through a haze of hard water spots and spider webs.
5. **Honey-do list shame.** It's been on the list for 18 months. Spouse keeps mentioning it. Doing it themselves means a Saturday on a ladder, sore back, streaky result, and an argument.
6. **Aging out of the ladder.** 55+ homeowner who used to do it themselves, knee gave out / spouse forbade it / kid in the ER from a fall last year. They're not climbing anymore, full stop.
7. **Listing the house.** Realtor said curb appeal sells, get the windows done before photos.
8. **Time-is-money outsourcing.** Two-earner household pulling $200K+, both work in Auburn Hills or downtown. Their Saturday is worth more than the cost of the service. Same logic as the lawn guy.
9. **Seasonal ritual.** Spring clean / fall clean is just what you do. They booked the mulch, the mower tune-up, the gutter guy. Windows complete the set.
10. **Recovery from a contractor mess.** Just had siding washed, roof done, painters, or new gutters installed. Everything is filthy from the work. They want one call to make the house feel finished.

White space your competitors aren't touching: **#1 hosting panic, #4 the view tax, #5 honey-do shame.** Every Metro Detroit competitor leads with "trusted family-owned since 1994." None of them speak to the actual emotional moment.

## 2. Top 5 objections

1. **"It'll just get dirty again in a week."** Michigan pollen, spring rain, lake-effect. Biggest objection by far. Kill it with a maintenance plan or a "lasts the season" promise.
2. **"$400 is a lot, I can just do it myself."** Costco squeegee, vinegar-and-Dawn TikTok, husband says "I got it." This is the DIY trap. Show them the ladder shot, the second-story dormers, the screen tracks they'd never touch.
3. **"Last guy ghosted me / showed up late / didn't do the screens."** This shows up in every competitor brief: no-shows, missed spots, surprise upcharges. Trust is fragile in this category. Lead with the guarantee.
4. **"Are they going to upsell me at the door / scratch my glass / damage my landscaping?"** Trust + competence. Especially for high-end homes in Bloomfield with leaded glass or custom transoms.
5. **"Do I really need the inside done?"** Half of them assume "windows" means outside only and price-shock when they hear it's both. Resolve in the ad: spell out interior + exterior + screens + tracks.

Your competitor reviews are SCREAMING three of these: streaks, no-shows, surprise upcharges. Lean into that gap hard.

## 3. Top 5 trigger moments

1. **First sunny morning after winter** (mid-March to early May). Coffee in hand, looking at the kitchen window, salt and grime visible for the first time in months.
2. **Hosting event booked on the calendar.** Graduation parties (May/June), Mother's Day, 4th of July, Thanksgiving, baby showers.
3. **Realtor walkthrough or listing prep.** "Get the windows done before we shoot photos."
4. **Gutter overflow during a thunderstorm.** Water sheeting down siding, soil washing into the foundation. They look up and realize the gutters AND the windows are a mess.
5. **Saturday-morning yard work guilt.** They just spent $600 on mulch and the yard looks great, but the house still looks tired. The contrast triggers the purchase.

## 4. Language they actually use

- **Hyperlocal:** "Up north" (cottage), "the Lodge," "I-696," "Telegraph," "Woodward." They name their suburb (Birmingham, Royal Oak, Rochester, Troy, Northville, Plymouth, Grosse Pointe, Bloomfield, Novi, Canton).
- **Pop can. Not soda.** Faygo, Vernors, Better Made if you want bonus points.
- **House talk:** "storm door," "bay window," "slider" (sliding door), "transoms," "dormers," "Pella" or "Andersen" windows, "vinyl siding," "brick colonial," "Tudor."
- **The grime vocabulary:** "tree sap," "pollen," "spider webs in the corners," "bug guts on the storm door," "bird sh*t on the bay window," "hard water spots," "dog nose smears," "kid handprints."
- **Honey-do list.** Universal. Use it.
- **Sports references work:** Lions, Tigers, Wings, Pistons. Spartan vs Wolverine if you want to risk it.
- **What they DON'T say:** "Squeaky clean." "Crystal clear." "Sparkling." Every corporate competitor uses these. They sound like a brochure. Real people say "I can actually see out of them" or "they don't look gross anymore."

Pattern across all three briefs: nobody sounds like a human. The "young hustlers" voice flagged in briefs #1 and #3 is wide open, in Detroit AND in LA. Confirms the direction.

## 5. Who they're comparing you to

**Direct competitors (Metro Detroit):**
- **Fish Window Cleaning** (franchise, uniformed crews, corporate)
- **Window Genie** (Neighborly franchise, similar)
- **Squeegee Squad** (local, family angle)
- **Sir Squeegee** (local, owner-operator)
- **Clear View** (budget flat-rate)

**Adjacent / alternative:**
- Pressure-washing companies that upsell windows
- Handyman services (TaskRabbit, Thumbtack guys)
- The neighborhood teenager whose dad owns a pressure washer
- The cleaning lady / Molly Maid asked to do interiors

**DIY alternatives (your real biggest competitor):**
- Costco / Home Depot squeegee + bucket
- Windex + paper towels
- The vinegar-and-Dawn TikTok trick
- Magic Eraser
- Garden hose with a brush attachment

**The "I'll get to it" alternative:** doing nothing for another year. This is the most common one. You're not competing with Fish; you're competing with procrastination.

## 6. Their day (the one that ends in a booking)

- **6:00-6:30 AM.** Wake up. Coffee maker in the kitchen. Standing at the sink or the island, looking out the back window at the deck. Notice the film. Again.
- **7:30 AM.** Drop the kids at school (Birmingham Public, Rochester Community, Bloomfield Hills, etc.). Drive past neighbors' houses, notice whose windows look done.
- **8:30 AM - 5:00 PM.** Work. Either WFH at the kitchen table (staring at the dirty slider all day) or commuting to Auburn Hills, Troy, downtown Detroit. Frustrating drive.
- **12:00-1:00 PM.** Lunch scroll. Instagram and Facebook on the phone. **This is where your ad hits.** They're not in research mode, they're in a 90-second distraction window. The ad has to stop the thumb in under 1.5 seconds and make them save it or DM.
- **5:30-6:30 PM.** Drive home, glance at the house pulling into the driveway. Curb appeal moment. The trim looks tired. Gutters have a streak of debris.
- **7:00-9:00 PM.** Dinner, kids, maybe a walk around the block. Notice neighbors' lights coming through clean windows. Theirs look hazy from the inside.
- **9:00-10:30 PM.** Couch scroll. Sunday night especially. **This is where the booking actually happens.** They search "window cleaning near me" or click the ad they saved at lunch. They compare 2-3 options on Google reviews. They text the spouse a screenshot. They book.
- **Saturday morning.** Costco run, Home Depot, mulch, lawn. The house gets visible attention. If they didn't book during the week, this is the second-chance window.

## Cross-brief synthesis (the three things that matter)

Three signals appear in MULTIPLE briefs and should drive every ad you make:

1. **Trust is the universal objection.** No-shows, streaks, surprise upcharges, missed screens. Show up in Detroit briefs, LA briefs, Vegas briefs. Lead with named crews, photo verification, guaranteed scheduling, flat-rate transparency. The biggest unowned position in the entire category.
2. **Everybody sounds corporate.** "Family-owned since 1994." "#1 rated." "Free estimates." Three markets, same wallpaper voice. The "young hustlers" / real-human voice is wide open. This is the single biggest creative lever you have.
3. **Nobody publishes prices.** Defined Cleaning in Vegas is the only competitor across three markets that posts numbers. Willis at $250-$450 has room to lead with "$X starting, posted up front, no surprise at the door" and instantly differentiate from every franchise hiding behind "free quote."

What's interesting: your existing **$100 off + free screen cleaning** promo is already bigger than anything in any of the three briefs. You're not just competing on offer, you're top of market. Make sure the ad says the number out loud. Don't bury it.
