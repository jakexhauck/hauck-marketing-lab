## 2. Positioning vs. Competitors (Read This First)

The Metro Detroit field is corporate and bland. We are not playing the same game.

| Competitor lane | Who runs it | What we DO NOT look like |
|---|---|---|
| Trusted franchise, uniformed crews | Fish Window Cleaning, Window Genie | No polo shirts, no stock smiles, no logo trucks in driveways. No "since [year]" tenure brag. |
| Local family / owner-operator | Squeegee Squad, Sir Squeegee | No grandpa-and-the-grandson narrative. No handshake-on-the-porch shots. |
| Budget flat-rate | Clear View | We are not the cheapest. We are the boldest offer, which is different. |
| Generic "free estimate" CTA | All of them | We name the offer in the CTA every time. Numbers, not vibes. |

**Our open flank (use all three):**
1. **Voice:** young hustlers — energetic, blue-collar, real Detroit. Sounds like a 24-year-old foreman, not a brand manager.
2. **Offer dominance:** $100 off + free screens is the largest visible offer in the market. Lead with the number, not the pitch.
3. **Same-week scheduling:** the #1 complaint across competitor reviews is no-shows and slow scheduling. We promise the opposite, in plain English.

**Hard "do not"s:**
- No fake urgency ("ACT NOW", countdown timers, "5 spots left this week" unless verifiable).
- No manufactured scarcity.
- No em dashes in copy. Commas, periods, parentheses only.
- No stock footage. No corporate B-roll. No drone shots of generic suburbs.
- No "family owned since…" tenure language. We're young. Lean in.

## 3. Target Audience

**Demographics:** Homeowners aged 25–65+, Metro Detroit, suburban single-family homes (Royal Oak, Birmingham, Bloomfield, Grosse Pointe, Sterling Heights, Northville, Plymouth, Livonia, Troy).

**Psychographics:** Time-poor, dual-income or busy parent, takes pride in the house but hasn't looked at the windows since fall. Has tried DIY at least once, hated it, gave up. Has been quoted by a franchise and found them slow or overpriced.

**Top pain points we hit:**
1. Detroit winter salt + road film coating glass.
2. Hard water spotting from city tap.
3. Spring pollen layer, especially near wooded lots and lakes.
4. Too busy, too time-consuming, kids/work/life.
5. Genuine safety concern with second-story and high windows.
6. DIY streaks that look worse than not cleaning.
7. Quiet embarrassment about curb appeal, especially before guests/events.

**Top desires we promise:**
1. Crystal clear, streak-free, light flooding back in.
2. Trusted crew that actually shows up, on time, this week.
3. No hidden fees, no at-door upcharges (a documented competitor failure).
4. Visible curb appeal before a party, listing photos, or holidays.

## 4. The Three Concepts

### CONCEPT A — "Detroit Salt Tax" (30-sec vertical video)

**Angle:** Problem Agitation (Category 3)
**Format:** 30-sec UGC-style vertical video, 9:16, 1080x1920
**DSL structure:** Standard (Disrupt → Story → Landing)

**Hook (0:00–0:03):**
> "Your windows haven't been clean since November. Detroit salt saw to that."

**Story (0:03–0:23):**
- Tight close-up of crusted, salt-streaked exterior glass. Real Detroit driveway, real winter grime.
- Cut to crew member (early 20s, hoodie, Carhartt, no logo polo) walking up to the house with a water-fed pole.
- Voiceover, conversational, fast: "Salt, road film, hard water spots, last fall's pollen. Three layers of Detroit caked on every pane. We run filtered water through a three-stage system, no soap residue, no streaks. Done in an afternoon."
- Cut sequence: pole on the second-story, screens popping off and getting cleaned on the lawn, squeegee inside, sunlight hitting the kitchen floor for the first time in months.

**Landing (0:23–0:30):**
- Crew member to camera, hands wet: "$100 off, free screen cleaning, on your driveway within the week. Free quote takes a minute. Link's right there."
- End frame: bold sans-serif text over clean glass — **$100 OFF + FREE SCREENS. METRO DETROIT.**

**Visual direction:**
- Handheld, slightly raw. Phone-shot energy even if shot on a real camera.
- Natural light only. Overcast Michigan morning ideal.
- Real homes, real driveways. No staged set.
- Color: cool blue-grey winter palette in the "before" frames, warmer golden light in the "after" frames. The transformation is the visual.

**Energy:** High but not frantic. Confident.

**Captions:** Burned in, bottom-third, sans-serif, white with black stroke. Keyword highlights ("$100 OFF", "FREE SCREENS", "WITHIN THE WEEK") in safety-yellow.

---

### CONCEPT B — "Let the Light Back In" (15-sec vertical short)

**Angle:** Transformation (Category 5)
**Format:** 15-sec vertical short, 9:16, 1080x1920
**DSL structure:** Short (Hook + Mini-story + CTA)

**Hook (0:00–0:03):**
> "This is how much light your house has been missing."

**Mini-story (0:03–0:11):**
- Split-screen or wipe transition: same window, same room, before and after.
- Before: muted, grey, sun blocked by film and spots.
- After: full sunlight pouring across hardwood floor, plants on the sill, kid's drawing on the fridge visible in the reflection.
- No voiceover. Just one sound design beat (a soft whoosh on the transition) and on-screen text:
  - "Three layers of Detroit on every window."
  - "One afternoon to fix it."

**Landing (0:11–0:15):**
- End card, full-bleed photo of clean window with sunlight, text overlay:
  - **$100 OFF + FREE SCREEN CLEANING**
  - **METRO DETROIT • BOOKED THIS WEEK**
  - **Free quote → [link]**

**Visual direction:**
- Cinematic but achievable. Tripod or gimbal, not handheld.
- The transformation is the entire creative. Camera does not move; the window does the work.
- Color grade: deepen the "before" shadows, lift the "after" highlights. Don't fake it; the contrast should already be there from a real clean.

**Energy:** Calm, satisfying. The Andromeda counterpoint to Concept A's high-energy.

**Captions:** Minimal, large, centered. No safety-yellow on this one — keep it premium-feeling to contrast Concept A.

---

### CONCEPT C — "Biggest Offer in Metro Detroit" (5-card static carousel)

**Angle:** Offer-led / Social Proof hybrid (Categories 2 + 6)
**Format:** 5-card static carousel, 1:1, 1080x1080 each
**Use case:** Feed, retargets cold viewers of A and B, also stands alone as cold

**Card 1 — Hook card:**
- Bold sans-serif, white text on deep navy:
  - **$100 OFF + FREE SCREEN CLEANING**
  - Small line under: "The biggest window cleaning offer in Metro Detroit. Not exaggerating."
- Tiny footer: "Swipe →"

**Card 2 — Competitive contrast (without naming names):**
- Header: "What everyone else is offering"
- Three line items, struck through:
  - ~~$25 off~~
  - ~~$50 off~~
  - ~~"Call for a quote"~~
- Footer: "Then there's us."

**Card 3 — The offer, plainly:**
- "$100 off your first clean."
- "Free screen cleaning (most charge $5–$10 per screen)."
- "Quote takes 60 seconds. No at-door upcharges."
- Small icons next to each line, no clip-art energy, hand-drawn linework.

**Card 4 — Proof:**
- "Hundreds of Metro Detroit homes."
- "Years on the road."
- "Licensed and insured."
- "Three-stage water filtration — no soap, no streaks, no spots."
- Bottom: one real review screenshot or quote (designer to source from existing reviews; if none available, leave a placeholder labeled `[INSERT VERIFIED REVIEW]` and flag back to Jake).

**Card 5 — CTA card:**
- "Free quote. Booked within the week."
- Big button graphic: **Get My Quote →**
- Footer: "Metro Detroit • Residential • Commercial • Screens • Gutters"

**Visual direction:**
- Editorial poster energy. Strong type hierarchy, generous white space, one accent color (suggest a deep Detroit navy + a single squeegee-yellow accent).
- Sans-serif throughout, weight 600+ for headlines, 400 for body.
- No italic serif anywhere.
- Subtle texture (paper grain or matte film) is fine; no gradients, no glossy buttons.

**Energy:** Confident, transparent, slightly cocky. The card 2 contrast is the whole pitch.
