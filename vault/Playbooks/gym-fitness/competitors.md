The fitness landscape splits cleanly between big-box and boutique. Different angles win in each.

Big-box competitors (Planet Fitness, LA Fitness, 24 Hour Fitness):
- Lead on $10/mo and "judgment-free" positioning. Hard to beat on price; beat them on coaching and community.
- Run generic "no contract" creative with stock gym photos.
- Differentiate against by showing real coaching and real results, not access to equipment.

Boutique competitors (Orangetheory, F45, CrossFit, SoulCycle, Pure Barre):
- Lead on a branded modality. Run high-production studio creative.
- Their CPL is higher, their audience is pre-sold on the format.
- Differentiate against by leaning into community over modality, owner story over corporate uniformity, programming flexibility over the rigid same-class-every-day format.

Independent gyms running boosted posts:
- Mostly not in the auction at any volume. Easy market share to capture.
- They tend to post member photos with no hook, no offer, no CTA. Just show up with a real ad.

What every gym runs and looks the same:
- "Get fit in 2026" January creative
- $10/mo or $1 first month promos
- Generic shots of weight racks and treadmills
- Class schedule carousels with no narrative

Lanes to own:
- Comeback / post-injury / post-baby messaging (almost nobody addresses this directly)
- Specific result claims with proof (12 lbs in 8 weeks, real testimonial)
- Founder / coach story for boutique studios
- Family-friendly programming for parents
- "First week free, no card" as the lowest-friction CTA in the auction
