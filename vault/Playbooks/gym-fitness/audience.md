Local gym and boutique fitness audience splits into three buying windows.

Primary: adults 25 to 45, household income $50k+, currently sedentary or 3-to-6-months out of a routine. Big enrollment moments: January (resolutions), late April (summer panic), September (back-to-school rhythm reset). Want results without becoming "gym people." Hate the meathead aesthetic, hate feeling watched, hate contracts.

Secondary: post-injury or post-baby comeback. 28 to 50, more cautious, wants coaching not just access. Will pay 2-3x more for a small-group studio over a $10/mo big-box. Trigger: doctor said something, hit a number on the scale, can't keep up with kids.

Tertiary: existing gym hoppers, 22 to 35. Cancelled their last gym for boredom or commute. Easy to convert with a 7-day pass; hard to retain past month 2.

Top objections: cost, time, intimidation, "I should get in shape first before joining," past failed memberships. The intimidation objection beats the cost objection in surveys.

Language: "get back in shape," "lose the last 20," "wedding in 6 months," "doctor said," "want to feel strong again," "not a gym person," "judgment-free," "doesn't require a contract."

Daily routine: scroll Instagram on the couch at 8-10 PM (highest-converting window for gyms), gym browse during lunch breaks 12-1 PM. Phone-first audience, mobile checkout is non-negotiable.
