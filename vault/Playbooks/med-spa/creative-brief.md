Visual style for med-spa creatives.

Quiet luxury. Real clinic, real injector, real patient. Phone-shot for testimonials and behind-the-scenes; controlled studio lighting for hero before-and-after slabs. Never lean into the "spa with cucumber slices" cliche; that audience is no longer there.

Patient photography: real patients with signed releases, lit evenly to avoid the cheap before-and-after lighting trick. Show subtle, believable results. Aggressive transformations look fake and trigger skepticism, even when real.

Color palette anchors: soft neutrals (sand, bone, dusty rose, eucalyptus), one accent color from the brand. Never harsh white-on-white. Logo bottom-right, never centered, never oversized.

On-camera talent: the actual injector or RN, in clinic uniform, with credentials lower-thirded on screen. Real patients in their own clothes, in their own homes for the most-trusted variant.

Sound: ambient, soft, never spa-flute. Music should match the clinic's brand (sometimes minimal indie, sometimes elevated commercial). Voiceover only by clinic staff or patient, never a hired narrator.

Do-nots:
- No stock-photo "woman touching face in soft light"
- No before-and-after with exaggerated lighting difference
- No "REVERSE TIME" or "LOOK YOUNGER" all-caps headlines
- No flute-and-bamboo "spa vibes" production aesthetics
- No model who is obviously 22 (audience is 35+)
- No syringe close-ups (triggers pain-objection)
- No comparison-to-Botox-Karen punching at competitors
- No medical-claim language ("cures wrinkles," "permanent results") that violates Meta ad policy
- No "ladies only" framing that excludes the male cohort
