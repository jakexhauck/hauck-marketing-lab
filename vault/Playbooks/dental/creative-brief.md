Visual style for dental creatives.

Real-photo first. Phone-shot reception desks, real hygienist faces, real patients in chairs (with consent). Lighting should feel like the actual office, not a stock studio. Warm neutrals, off-white walls, soft daylight. No clinical sterile blue-white that screams "medical office."

Smile shots: real teeth with minor imperfection. Avoid the airbrushed veneer-grin that triggers BS detectors. Before-and-after slabs work; keep the "before" honest, not exaggerated.

Color palette anchors: warm white, soft sage or muted teal as accent, never harsh medical blue. Logo bottom-right, never centered.

On-camera talent: hygienist or dentist (not actors), 25 to 50, friendly demeanor, scrubs in brand color. Real names in the caption.

Do-nots:
- No clip-art teeth icons
- No corporate stock "doctor in white coat with crossed arms"
- No "PERFECT SMILE" headline screams
- No before/after where the before is yellowed beyond reality
- No baby-talking to patients in voiceover
- No fake reviews or testimonials without a real patient release
- No comparison to "those other dentists" — punches sideways and reads desperate
