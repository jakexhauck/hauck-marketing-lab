After they've trained, most of the best organizations I've worked with, including Grants, they do a morning meeting. So at 9 a. m. sharp in Grants' organization, all the people in the organization were on a morning meeting. They would walk them through, not just a bunch of bullshit, okay? They would walk them through the statistics of the organization. They would be transparent about everything. They would show people who are doing really well. They'd have those people share lessons with the rest of the organization. They would call out people who are doing bad and they would ask, hey, why are you doing bad? Can anybody here help you do better? They would make all of that transparent and public. It wouldn't just be a bunch of bullshit once again where it's like, all right guys, here at the morning meeting, really excited for the day. Let's go out there and crush it. It wouldn't say stupid shit like that They'd say real actionable data that can move the organization and the people that are part of the organization to a better place before they go out there and do their jobs for the day. Now the sales team, specifically in Grant's office, what they would do next is they would role-play. They'd spend about 30 to 45 minutes pairing up with one another, and they would role-play. The sales management would walk around the people who are role-playing. They'd listen in, they'd sometimes chime in, and they would add lessons and value when needed. Now to be clear, that's what makes great sales organizations. Before they even actually take calls, notice they did three things that would make them better for the day.
