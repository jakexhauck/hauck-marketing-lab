01Colour

Basalt, brass, moss, warm stone.

Four colours and their working variants. The whole palette comes off the website, so an ad and the landing page it points at look like the same company. Brass is the one that gets people into trouble, so read the second half of this section before you set any brass text.

#0E1311

Basalt

The dark surface. Near-black with green in it, never pure black. Backgrounds, scrims, text on light.

The brass trap

Brass #C8974B on white measures 2.63:1. That is well under the 4.5:1 a person needs to read a caption, and under the 3:1 that even a big headline needs. It looks fine on a desktop monitor in a bright room and disappears on a phone outdoors, which is exactly where these ads get seen.

So: brass is a fill. Put brass behind basalt text, not the other way round. If you genuinely need brass-coloured words on a light background, use #876633, or #AB8140 at display size only.

How the colours split the work

  • Basalt carries the ad. Dark ads outperform light ones in a feed because the feed itself is white or near-black, and a mid-tone photo with a basalt block reads as a designed thing rather than a post.
  • Brass is the one bright thing. One brass element per ad. A brass button and a brass headline and a brass border is three, and none of them get looked at.
  • Moss is a substitute, not an addition. Reach for it when the photo is already full of gold light and brass would vanish into it.
  • Warm wash, never pure white. A pure white panel next to these greens reads blue and cheap. #F7F6F2 or #EFEDE6.
  • No colour that is not on this page. Not a red urgency banner, not a yellow star, not a blue "verified" tick. If an ad needs a fifth colour, it needs a different idea.

02Type

Two faces. Tight tracking. Nothing else.

Plus Jakarta Sans at 800 for anything big, Inter for everything small. The negative tracking on the headlines is what stops a large sans from reading as a phone's default font, so do not skip it.

Display face

Plus Jakarta Sans

Weight 800 for headlines and the eyebrow. Weight 700 for a subhead or a button label. Tracking -0.02em. Line height 1.08 to 1.15 on an ad, tighter than the website, because ad headlines are short.

Text face

Inter

Weight 400 to 600 for the support line, the price, the fineprint. Never 800: that is the display face's job, and two heavy sans faces in one frame look like a mistake.

The scale, as a share of canvas width

Every Meta size we run is 1080px wide, so pegging type to the width gives one scale that works on the square, the portrait and the story without redoing the maths. The px column is the value at 1080.

Nothing goes below 1.9% of width. At 1080 that is 21px, which is about 6px on a phone. Below it, nobody reads it and it just makes the ad look busy.

Type rules

  • Two sizes per ad, three at the very most. A headline, a support line, a button. Every extra size costs you a beat of attention.
  • Never stretch, condense, or outline the type. If it does not fit, cut a word.
  • No drop shadows on text. Contrast comes from the surface behind it. See the next section.
  • Sentence case for headlines. ALL CAPS is reserved for the eyebrow, where the letter-spacing is doing the work.
  • No italics. The brand does not have an italic voice, and the display face's italic reads as a different family.

03Text on ads

Where the words go, and how few of them.

An ad gets somewhere between half a second and a second and a half. Everything in this section is about making the one idea survive that.

The three canvases and their dead zones

Hatched red is covered by the platform's own interface. Nothing readable goes there. The dashed box is where the message lives.

The safe zones are not a suggestion

On a Reel, Meta reserves roughly the top 250px and the bottom 640px of a 1080 × 1920 frame for its own interface. A headline placed low in the frame is not "at the bottom of the ad", it is behind the caption and the profile name. Stories are less greedy, about 250px top and 340px bottom, so a creative built to the Reels safe zone runs in both without a second export.

How much text

7

Words, headline max

Over two lines at the most. Around 22 characters per line.

12

Words, support line max

One line. If it wraps to three, it is a paragraph and it will not be read.

25

Words on the whole canvas

Everything visible added together, button and fineprint included.

1

Idea per ad

One offer, one proof point, one action. Two offers is zero offers.

The long version of the pitch belongs in the primary text field above the image, where there is room for it and where nobody has to read it to understand the picture.

Placing the text block

  • Pick a third and commit to it. Top third or bottom third. Text floating in the middle of a photo has nothing holding it and reads as a stock template.
  • On 9:16, the upper-middle. It is the only region both Stories and Reels leave alone.
  • Left-align everything. Centred type on a photo is the single most reliable tell of an amateur ad. The only exception is a single word.
  • Text and photo subject do not overlap. If the words sit over the lawn, they are competing with the thing you are selling.
  • The button goes last in reading order. Bottom of the text block, not the top, not floating off on its own.

Text over a photo: the scrim rule

White text needs the pixels underneath it at or below roughly 18% luminance to stay readable. A photo of a sunlit lawn is nowhere near that. So the photo gets darkened where the text sits, every time, with no exceptions and no shadows used as a substitute.

Scrim recipes

  • Gradient: basalt #0E1311 at 90% opacity at the text edge, falling to 0% by 60% across the frame. Text sits in the 90% end.
  • Flat: basalt at 55% minimum, 62% if the photo has bright sky or concrete in it.
  • Solid block: a basalt panel with the text inside it, photo untouched. The cleanest option and the one that survives any photo.
  • Brass text never goes over a photo. Not with a scrim, not with a shadow. Brass over a photo is 2 point something to one against the bright bits, whatever you do to it. Brass on an ad means a solid basalt or brass surface.
  • A drop shadow is not a scrim. It fakes contrast at the letter's edge and fails on the letter's interior. Every phone screenshot of a shadowed headline looks muddy.
  • No blur behind text. It looks like the photo is out of focus, which for a landscaping company is the wrong signal entirely.

04Logo and marks

Small, quiet, and in the same corner every time.

The logo is not the ad. It is the signature. Its job is to be there when someone who already saw the last ad scrolls past this one.

The mark

MADE BETTER.

Served from the same Google Drive file the website uses. If it does not render above, the file is not publicly readable and needs a proper asset dropped into this folder.

The wordmark

MADE BETTER.

Plus Jakarta Sans 800, tracking -0.02em, all caps, with the full stop in brass. The full stop is the whole idea: it is the "better" being a finished thing. Never drop it, never colour it the same as the words.

Sizing and clear space

Clear space

dashed line = nothing crosses it

MADE BETTER.

The gap between the mark and the dashed line is the clear space. Measure it as half the mark's own height, so it scales with the logo. Nothing crosses the dashed line: no headline, no photo subject, no canvas edge.

Minimum size on a 1080 canvas

  • Logo mark: 90px tall. Roughly 8% of the canvas width. Below that the detail turns to mush at feed scale.
  • Wordmark: 28px tall. The fallback when the frame is too tight for the mark.
  • Maximum: 140px. A logo bigger than the headline is a business-card, not an ad.
  • One placement per campaign. Bottom-left or top-left, chosen once, kept across every ad in the set.

  • Never recolour the mark to match a photo, a season, or a holiday.
  • Never add a stroke, glow, bevel or shadow to make it show up. If it needs help showing up, it needs a chip behind it.
  • Never rotate or place it at an angle.
  • Never put the logo and the wordmark in the same corner. Pick one per ad.
  • Never let the logo be the largest element. The offer is.

05Pre-flight

Run this before anything goes live.

Nine checks. If one fails, the ad does not ship, it gets fixed.

  1. Every colour on the canvas is on this page. Four brand colours and their listed variants. Nothing borrowed from a stock template.
  2. No brass text on a light background, and no brass text over a photo. If brass words are on light, they are #876633, or #AB8140 above 40px.
  3. All text sits inside the safe zone for its canvas. 9:16 in particular: nothing readable in the top 250px or the bottom 640px.
  4. On 4:5, the headline and logo are inside the centre square. The profile grid crops to 1:1 and will cut anything at the top or bottom edge.
  5. Text over a photo has a real scrim, not a drop shadow. Basalt at 55% flat, or a 90% to 0% gradient.
  6. Under 25 words on the whole canvas, one idea, one action. Headline at seven words or fewer, over two lines at most.
  7. Nothing smaller than 21px at 1080 wide. Fineprint included. If it will not fit above the floor, delete it.
  8. The logo has its clear space and is not the biggest thing in the frame. 90px minimum, 140px maximum, same corner as the rest of the set.
  9. Viewed at thumbnail size on an actual phone, the offer still reads. Shrink it to 25% on screen. If the headline goes, the ad goes.
